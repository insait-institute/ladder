LADDER

How well can today’s best AI models control a robot?

Success vs cost

Each dot is one AI model. Higher means it finishes more tasks. Further right means it costs more. Models marked Lite are scored on the 10 LADDER-Lite tasks.

Success comes at a price

GPT-6 Astra costs $6.49 per finished task, 20 times more than Gemini 3.7 Flash via PSP-Agent, which finishes 42.8% of the tasks.

New models on LADDER-Lite

On LADDER-Lite, Gemini 4 Argon finishes 55.9% at $1.33 per finished task; Claude Opus 5.5 finishes 49.0% at $7.02 per finished task. GPT-6 Astra finishes 62.5% at $6.62.

Why this matters

2× speed

“Put the banana, both oranges, the lemon, the lime, the avocado, the onion, the lychee, the pumpkin and the pomegranate in the bin”

Looks easy? Not for robots.

Putting ten fruits and vegetables in a bin is child’s play for a person. For a robot it is still very hard: it has to find each object, pick it up, carry it and put it down, ten times in a row, without a single mistake.

Robots that can reliably handle everyday objects are one of the biggest unsolved challenges in AI. LADDER measures how far today’s best AI models have come.

Far from solvedThe best AI model we tested finishes 63.3% of the tasks.
Longer is harderWith 8 to 10 objects to move, it finishes 34.8%.

How LADDER works

Long task · 9 objects

4× speed

“Put the banana, the lemon and the lime on the plate and put both oranges, the avocado, the onion, the lychee and the pumpkin in the bin”

The same table and the same two containers. The long task only adds more objects to sort, so any drop in success comes from the length alone. One slip anywhere and the whole task fails. Both clips are GPT-Policy, the best system we tested.

145 everyday tasks

From 1 to 10 objects: putting things on a plate or in a bin, in a set order, moving them from one place to another, or leaving some where they are.

Checked automatically

Every task runs in a robot simulator that checks where each object ends up. A task only counts when everything is in the right place.

The longer the task, the more often robots fail

Share of tasks finished, the three simulators weighed equally. Hover the chart to read the values. Click a name to show or hide it.

Simulation matches the real world

We ran the same kinds of tasks on two real robots. The models rank in the same order as in simulation.
